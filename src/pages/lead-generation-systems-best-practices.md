## Detailed explanation

Implementing lead generation systems in Melbourne requires a structured approach to ensure clear handoffs and practical checks. Start by defining the owner and their responsibilities.

Next, identify the required inputs, such as lead sources, contact information, and lead scoring criteria. Clearly outline the expected outcome, which could be a qualified lead, a sales-ready lead, or a lead assigned to a specific sales rep.

Establish decision criteria for lead qualification, such as budget, authority, needs, and timeline (BANT). This will help ensure that leads are qualified consistently and accurately.

To measure the effectiveness of your lead generation system, choose a metric that reflects the improvement you want to see. This could be lead conversion rate, sales qualified lead (SQL) rate, or time to lead qualification. Regularly track and analyze this metric to identify trends and areas for improvement.

## Examples

For instance, an independent accounting firm in Melbourne might use the following lead generation system:

Owner: Marketing Manager
Required Inputs: Lead source (e.g., website, referral, trade show), contact information, lead score
Expected Outcome: Lead assigned to a specific sales rep with a lead score of 70 or higher
Decision Criteria: BANT (Budget, Authority, Needs, Timeline) and lead score
Metric: Time to lead qualification (target: less than 24 hours)

## Common mistakes

Some common mistakes to avoid when implementing lead generation systems in Melbourne include:

1. **Unclear or shared ownership**: This can lead to confusion, delays, and inconsistent lead qualification.

2. **Incomplete or inconsistent data**: Inaccurate or missing data can result in poor lead quality and wasted sales efforts.

3. **Inconsistent lead qualification**: Without clear decision criteria, leads may be qualified inconsistently, leading to missed opportunities or wasted resources.

4. **No tracking or analysis of metrics**: Without regular tracking and analysis, it's difficult to identify trends, measure improvement, and make data-driven decisions.
